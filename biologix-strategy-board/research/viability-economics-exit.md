# Peptide viability, economics, and exit reality

**Decision date:** July 26, 2026  
**Use:** Noli investment and operating decision, not a forecast or valuation opinion.

## Decision in 30 seconds

**Conditional go, not a company-wide commitment.**

- The opportunity can be economically large. The molecule is not the moat.
- A consumer RUO creator funnel is a poor fit for Noli. The viable lanes are a counsel-cleared qualified-research B2B model, a separately governed licensed clinical model, or a media and data layer.
- A 50-order cohort is a learning asset, not a business. In the base model it produces only about **$2,800 monthly CM2 before fixed overhead**.
- At 500 monthly orders, base CM2 is about **$27,600 before fixed overhead**. That may still be breakeven or worse after legal, quality, management, insurance, and compliance cost.
- At 5,000 monthly orders, base CM2 is about **$276,000 before fixed overhead**, but the base cash buffer is roughly **$1.35 million**.
- A real $50,000-per-day business could support a $15 million price. A phone screen, seller asking price, or BHW post does not prove one.

The right move is to buy information first: reconcile Braden's evidence, clear one legal lane, and run one 50-order truth cohort. Do not buy inventory at scale, build software, recruit 250 creators, or value a company before those gates.

## Could $15 million still be real?

Yes, economically. It is not supported by the cited BHW thread.

If a business really settles **$50,000 every day**, annual revenue is about **$18.25 million**:

- $15 million is **0.82x revenue**.
- At a 30% normalized owner-earnings margin, annual earnings are $5.48 million and $15 million is **2.74x earnings**.
- At a 10% margin, earnings are $1.83 million and $15 million is **8.22x earnings**, an aggressive price for a high-risk merchant.
- At the BizBuySell median 3.28x earnings multiple, a $15 million exit needs **$4.57 million annual owner earnings**, or 25.1% of $18.25 million revenue.
- At the 3.99x upper quartile, it needs **$3.76 million earnings**, or 20.6%.

That means Alex's intuition is reasonable: if the screens represent settled revenue, if the volume persists, and if normalized owner earnings really exceed 20%, a $15 million value is plausible.

The evidence still needs reconciliation. Screens can show orders, authorizations, GMV, or attributed revenue. They do not show reserves, refunds, disputes, inventory, taxes, legal exposure, supplier liabilities, or cash.

The current $14.5 million listing itself contains a useful warning. It claims $220 AOV and roughly 250 daily orders, which implies about $55,000 daily and $20.1 million annual revenue at a constant rate, yet the same listing reports $12.33 million revenue. A recent run-rate could explain the gap, but the listing does not reconcile it. It also reports SDE and EBITDA as the identical number.

## CM2 model

**CM2 means revenue after landed product and lot testing, fulfillment, processing expense, refunds and recovery, demand cost, and variable support. It excludes salaries, legal, insurance, quality management, clinical partners, tax, and general overhead.**

Net AOV is after checkout discount. Demand cost includes creator commission or paid acquisition, but not an additional customer discount.

| Per-order input | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net AOV | $110.00 | $150.00 | $200.00 |
| Product, packaging, lot release | 28% | 20% | 13% |
| Fulfillment and shipping | $16.00 | $12.00 | $9.00 |
| Processing expense | 10% | 6.5% | 4.5% |
| Refund, dispute, reship provision | 8% | 4% | 2% |
| Creator or paid demand cost | 35% | 22% | 12% |
| Variable support and technology | $6.00 | $4.00 | $2.50 |
| **CM2 per order** | **-$1.10** | **$55.25** | **$125.50** |
| **CM2 margin** | **-1.0%** | **36.8%** | **62.8%** |

### Monthly result by order volume

| Orders per month | Bear GMV / CM2 | Base GMV / CM2 | Bull GMV / CM2 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 | $5,500 / **-$55** | $7,500 / **$2,763** | $10,000 / **$6,275** |
| 500 | $55,000 / **-$550** | $75,000 / **$27,625** | $100,000 / **$62,750** |
| 5,000 | $550,000 / **-$5,500** | $750,000 / **$276,250** | $1,000,000 / **$627,500** |

This is why an 80% vial margin can coexist with a weak company. The most powerful public comparison is Hims: 74% 2025 gross margin became 14% adjusted EBITDA margin and 5% net margin after 39% marketing, 12% operations and support, 7% technology, and 12% G&A.

## Base cash requirement

The cash model uses:

- two months of landed product, testing allocation, and fulfillment
- one month of demand and variable support
- a steady 10% rolling reserve held for six months
- a quality, legal, insurance, systems, and contingency floor that rises with scale

| Monthly orders | Two-month inventory and fulfillment | Demand and support | Steady reserve | Quality/legal/contingency floor | **Base cash buffer** |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 | $4,200 | $1,850 | $4,500 | $40,000 | **$50,550** |
| 500 | $42,000 | $18,500 | $45,000 | $100,000 | **$205,500** |
| 5,000 | $420,000 | $185,000 | $450,000 | $300,000 | **$1,355,000** |

The reserve builds over the hold period rather than being due on day one. At 5% held 90 days, steady trapped cash is 15% of one month's GMV. At 15% held 180 days, it is 90%. At base GMV, that reserve range is:

- 50 orders: $1,125 to $6,750
- 500 orders: $11,250 to $67,500
- 5,000 orders: $112,500 to $675,000

The buffer does not include an acquisition price, large regulatory defense, recall, or a major processor shutdown.

## What earns a strong exit

The base case is a main-street ecommerce multiple, not a venture multiple. A buyer pays more when the company has:

1. A transferable, counsel-cleared operating lane with no contradiction between product, audience, claims, and delivery.
2. Twelve or more months of reconciled orders, settlements, bank cash, tax returns, and normalized earnings.
3. Real first, second, and third-order cohorts with low involuntary churn, not a seller's LTV claim.
4. Written processor approval, clean chargebacks, manageable reserves, and a compliant approved continuity path.
5. Lot-level chain of custody, release specifications, failed-lot records, supplier redundancy, and insurance.
6. Diversified demand with no dominant creator, ad account, keyword, platform, or founder.
7. CM2 by SKU, creator, channel, and cohort, plus inventory and working-capital discipline.
8. A real management team and SOPs that reduce founder dependence.
9. Owned consented audience, content, data, and software that remain valuable if a hero product changes.
10. A licensed clinical, testing, wholesale, or data capability that a strategic buyer cannot cheaply recreate.

A buyer discounts:

- consumer human-use behavior wrapped in RUO language
- regulatory warning, disputed molecule status, or creator claims
- rotating or misclassified processors and many disposable ad accounts
- high reserves, frozen settlements, crypto or cash that does not reconcile
- one supplier, one lab, one creator, one paid channel, or one founder
- COAs that do not match released inventory or omit relevant attributes
- revenue screenshots, attributed ROAS, and seller LTV without cohorts
- gross margin described as net margin
- owner add-backs that make SDE and EBITDA appear identical
- weak customer support, refund, reship, and adverse-event processes

The 10.7x supplement-sector figure is a north star for mature compliant brands, not a peptide-store comp. The Eucalyptus structure is more instructive: strategic value can be large, but sophisticated buyers defer and condition much of the consideration on future performance.

## Noli leverage and opportunity cost

Noli is the peptide company. The OVO entities below are optional support layers, not alternate names for Noli.

| Noli asset or support layer | Real leverage | Risk or cost | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OVO Talent** | Existing creator trust, partner operations, contracts, briefing, attribution, and payout communication can reduce time to a clean acquisition test | Commission can consume 10% to 20% or more; uncontrolled health content can create legal and payment evidence | Five opt-in partners, one approved brief, one offer. Talent is not merchant of record and does not influence clinical decisions |
| **OVO Academy** | A supervised bench can handle evidence verification, creator onboarding, CRM hygiene, support QA, and weekly reporting | Training and supervision consume capacity that could serve core clients; apprentices cannot provide medical advice or invent claims | Use only after the manual workflow works and only on approved materials |
| **OVO Labs** | Attribution, settlement reconciliation, cohort intelligence, content learning, and payout QA can become transferable operating IP | Premature software consumes focus before the business truth is known | Spreadsheet first. Build only when the same manual pain repeats |
| **Alex and Noli brand** | Faster trust, recruiting, and partner access | The reputational downside is larger than the pilot revenue | Ring-fenced Noli and Alex does not make product or outcome claims |

The opportunity cost is decisive. Base CM2 at 500 orders is only $27,625 before fixed overhead. Noli should not degrade OVO Talent, OVO Academy, or existing OVO delivery for that result.

The internal hurdle is:

> Incremental settled CM2 per creator, operator hour, and Alex hour must beat the same constrained capacity deployed in OVO's existing core.

If the peptide project still requires Alex as chief support agent, compliance reviewer, processor negotiator, and fulfillment fixer at 500 orders, it has not earned the right to scale.

## Stage gates

### Before order one

- Specialist counsel clears one lane, one entity structure, one eligible offer, audience, claims, compensation, and data flow.
- The acquiring bank approves the exact business and catalog in writing.
- Supplier, independent lab, lot-release specification, 3PL, insurance, support, recall, and refund processes are contracted and tested.
- Braden's numbers remain diligence inputs, not Noli forecasts.

### At 50 delivered orders

Advance only if:

- authorization reconciles to settlement and bank cash
- settled CM2 is at least 30%
- on-time delivery is at least 95%
- refund, dispute, and reship provision is no more than 5%
- every lot and order is traceable
- there are zero unresolved critical quality or claims violations

### At 500 monthly orders

Advance only after three clean consecutive months:

- realized second-order contribution supports LTV/CAC, without projected renewals
- blended demand cost is 25% or less
- top-creator and top-channel concentration are falling
- support and payout SLAs hold without Alex
- processor reserves and inventory remain inside the cash plan
- Talent and Academy core delivery have not deteriorated

### At 5,000 monthly orders

Require:

- at least twelve months of clean financial and quality records
- independently reviewed earnings and tax reconciliation
- supplier, lab, processor, fulfillment, and leadership redundancy
- the roughly $1.35 million base liquidity buffer
- a board-level decision on whether the asset is a cash-flow business, a clinical platform, infrastructure, or an exit candidate

## Best next move

Run two tracks for 30 days:

1. **Diligence Braden:** obtain read-only order exports, processor statements, bank settlements, supplier invoices, lot tests, inventory roll-forward, refunds, affiliate payouts, taxes, and any actual LOI.
2. **Clear the Noli lane:** specialist counsel and prospective counterparties define one lawful offer and exactly what creators, Academy operators, and Noli systems may do.

If both pass, run five partners to 50 delivered orders. If either fails, do not rationalize it with margin screenshots.

**Final judgment:** the upside is real enough to pursue seriously, but only through disciplined proof. The fastest route is not the biggest catalog or all 250 creators. It is one cleared offer, five partners, 50 delivered orders, reconciled cash, then the next zero.
