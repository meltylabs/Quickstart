The answer in one paragraph

Alex’s core thesis is right: there is a real, unusually leveraged demand engine here, and Biologix appears dramatically underbuilt relative to the money Braden claims it produces. I found 30 active U.S.-facing retatrutide seller candidates, at least 10 with credible operation by 2024 or earlier, and a live creator network that includes multiple accounts above 250,000 followers. The scarce asset is not the WordPress site or a source. It is trusted distribution, attribution, first-party customer data, lifecycle retention, quality/fulfillment credibility, and the operating system tying it together. But “still online” does not validate a loophole: warned sellers split among continued public retail, gated catalogs, removed products, migrated domains, and parked stores. The opportunity is commercially real; the durability and Braden-specific financial claims still require primary records.

What the market leaders are doing

The commercially mature pattern is:

social/search attention → creator trust or scientific-looking content → guide, Linktree, coaching, community, or podcast → coded product → coupon/referral → email/SMS, subscription, points, drops, and reorder

The better operators combine:

  • creator recruitment and codes;
  • SEO/content;
  • public batch reports;
  • strength and bundle ladders;
  • fast-shipping promises;
  • lifecycle email/SMS;
  • points or subscription;
  • review collection;
  • broad catalog cross-sell;
  • account qualification;
  • partner dashboards and payout processes.

The creator often owns the trust while the seller is replaceable. Dymitri’s six-vendor portfolio is the clearest example.

The central contradiction

The storefront commonly says:

  • research use only;
  • not for human consumption;
  • qualified researcher;
  • 21+;
  • batch tested.

The creator side commonly adds:

  • personal use;
  • transformation;
  • weight or appetite narrative;
  • source recommendation;
  • preparation/administration;
  • an exact code.

That division is commercially effective because the creator supplies the meaning and the seller closes the transaction. It is not evidence that the two layers are legally or operationally independent.

What “long-standing and still alive” actually looks like

There is no single survivor archetype:

  1. Open and explicit: Prime Peptides and PekCura continue public retatrutide offers.
  2. Gated: Xcel, Mile High, Pink Pony, and Prime Sciences moved public catalog access behind accounts/research access.
  3. Broader store survived but cited GLPs disappeared: SwissChems.
  4. Store persisted, then disappeared/parked: Summit Research.
  5. New/migrated identity: Core .st, Limitless .st, and uncertain Summit continuity.
  6. Closed: Peptide Sciences, Paradigm, and other historical names.

This is a churn market with enforcement delay—not a stable list of untouchable incumbents.

The most important correction to the “they must have figured it out” thesis

Common configurations include:

  • RUO footers and terms;
  • 18+/21+ gates;
  • researcher attestations;
  • account review;
  • coded names;
  • CAS numbers;
  • COAs;
  • affiliates on separate platforms;
  • migrated domains.

FDA has already cited sellers using these configurations. Their existence describes the current market surface; it does not demonstrate why a seller remains online or that an agency accepted the setup.

Why Braden can plausibly make much more than a harder-working service business

Biologix owns the customer transaction and markup:

  • customer pays the store directly;
  • organic or affiliate CAC can be low;
  • the same catalog can sell repeatedly;
  • product revenue is less directly tied to headcount than managed services;
  • the owner captures the transaction economics.

OVO Talent is a service layer:

  • someone else owns the final customer/product economics;
  • every creator and campaign adds management work;
  • revenue scales with labor and complexity;
  • OVO captures only part of the value it creates.

This is not evidence Braden is a better operator. It is evidence that he occupies a more leveraged position in the value chain.

What the public pages do not prove

  • Braden’s $1.5 million last-30-day claim.
  • A $90,000 day.
  • 80% margin.
  • Paid, fulfilled, net-of-refund orders.
  • Customer repeat rate.
  • True source and landed cost.
  • Affiliate productivity.
  • Cash available after processors, reserves, taxes, chargebacks, and inventory.
  • Product identity, sterility, chain of custody, or lot-to-customer matching.

Public stock or “sold” counters are seller-controlled fields, not accounting records.

The move

The best move remains:

  1. Enter through a paid operating/diligence sprint.
  2. Obtain complete order, settlement, customer, product, refund, processor, inventory, and affiliate data.
  3. Reconcile the financial claims.
  4. Reverse-engineer the word-of-mouth loop.
  5. Remove Braden from fulfillment.
  6. Build affiliate CRM, contracts, codes, attribution, payouts, and governance.
  7. Build lifecycle, referral, customer support, finance, and QA instrumentation.
  8. Negotiate meaningful economics only after the value baseline and risk surface are known.
  9. Put durable audience, media, software, data, community, and lawful-care infrastructure into a separate company Alex substantially owns.

  • Disclose every agency communication, warning, inquiry, detention, recall, or legal threat.

Bottom line

This is not a fake opportunity and it is not merely shiny-object syndrome. Alex has a plausible unfair advantage in relationships, creator operations, content, systems, and speed.

The mistake would be confusing:

  • a hot transaction with a durable company;
  • a public counter with verified revenue;
  • a creator code with a productive affiliate;
  • an old domain with a long-running operator;
  • an online storefront with evidence that its model has been accepted.

The right thesis is:

Use the hot cash engine to acquire ownership of durable distribution, customer data, operating systems, media, and community—then make sure Alex is paid as a principal for creating that durability.
