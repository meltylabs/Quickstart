05 / Analytical testing

“USA tested” can be true and still say nothing about origin

A laboratory report characterizes the submitted sample. Without independent sampling and chain of custody, it does not establish who manufactured it or whether every vial in the lot matches.

United States · Michigan TrustPointe Analytics Purity, identity, content, sterility, endotoxin, and related testing United States · Tennessee Freedom Diagnostics Appears on public peptide analytical reports United States · Arizona MZ Biolabs Public COA testing services United States · Illinois submission Kovera Labs Appears on public peptide analytical reports Czechia · Not USA testing Janoshik Analytical Widely used by gray-market vendors and buyer groups
Claim

99% purity

Usually describes the relative chromatographic purity of detected peptide peaks. It does not mean 99% of everything in the vial is the intended peptide.

Claim

Triple tested

No standard definition. It can mean three methods, three safety dimensions, or three laboratories. The underlying reports must define it.

Claim

USA tested

May prove only that a seller-selected vial reached a U.S. laboratory. It does not prove U.S. manufacture, random sampling, sterility, or lot-wide consistency.

Sampling: TrustPointe explains why one vial cannot establish a large lot’s quality.

Purity: TrustPointe’s purity note separates chromatographic purity from total vial composition.

Sterility: Its USP <71> service describes a 14–21 day process that may require several vials.

Published-COA dataset: A private 2026 Disclosed Labs analysis found high reported purity but materially weaker label-content and sterility coverage.

Purity versus endotoxin: TitrateLab reports that all sixty endotoxin-detected batches in its corpus passed purity, demonstrating that HPLC purity cannot substitute for microbial-safety testing.

Manufacturer quality gate: FDA’s GLP-1 API Import Alert 66-80 identifies the site, batch, method-validation, process-validation, OOS, stability, and microbial records expected in serious diligence.

Packaging similarity is not synthesis proof.

07 / Reference conclusions

The answers worth carrying forward

01
Best risk-adjusted Chinese direct-source target

Hybio. It has the strongest combined API and regulated finished-dose evidence, but its retatrutide remains R&D and commercial availability is not public.

02
Strongest API scale and RFQ challenger

Sinopep-Allsino. Keep it in the controlled RFQ, but do not infer price leadership without a comparable quote; require enhanced plant, data-integrity, and governance diligence before qualification.

03
Integrated one-site candidate, not the current winner

Fujian Genohope. Its API and sterile-fill footprint is real, but its inspection history, data-integrity observations, and disclosed ability to source external API make lot-level provenance essential.

04
No qualified gray-market direct source

HK, SRY, ZLZ, Yiwu Aozuo, and JEEP are storefront, trading, or aggregation operations rather than verified synthesis plants. None should be carried forward as “the factory.”

05
No defensible cheapest-factory answer

Public price lists are broker quotes, not auditable factory-gate pricing. MOQs, molecule, testing, fill, packaging, and intermediaries change the quote.

06
Direct-source proof is lot- and site-specific

Require the exact legal manufacturer and FEI or site address, original manufacturer batch number and CoA, authenticated chain of custody, subcontractor disclosure, audit rights, and a prohibition on undisclosed source changes.

07
No single broad-catalog factory meets every requirement

The best current structure is an integrated primary manufacturer plus a qualified second source. API synthesis and aseptic fill are distinct quality systems, and broad catalogs often involve aggregation.
