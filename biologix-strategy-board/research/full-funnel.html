Scope: Active U.S.-facing research-product sellers with a current retatrutide-labelled or clearly mapped offer, plus the strongest publicly verifiable creator-to-seller funnels.
Method: Public pages, public policy documents, public laboratory reports, public creator hubs and guides, public archives, and official FDA/DOJ records. No account was created, no purchase was made, and no private catalog or community was entered.

Executive answer

The market is more sophisticated than “put up a WordPress site and add a research-only footer,” but the sophistication is concentrated in distribution and conversion, not in the website itself.

The strongest operators layer:

  1. Search, social, creator, podcast, community, or referral traffic.
  2. A bridge asset that creates trust: a guide, Linktree, podcast episode, article library, review, or coaching community.
  3. A product page with a coded or explicit retatrutide offer.
  4. Batch reports, purity claims, fast-shipping language, and visible support.
  5. A price ladder: strengths, quantity breaks, subscriptions, coupons, free-shipping thresholds, or wholesale cases.
  6. A self-attestation, age gate, account review, or research questionnaire.
  7. Card, bank, and sometimes alternative payment routes.
  8. Email, SMS, account history, points, subscriptions, inventory alerts, and reorder offers.
  9. Affiliate links, creator codes, wholesale, or partner portals that restart the loop.

The commercial insight is straightforward:

The seller owns the transaction, while creators and content supply trust, meaning, and customer acquisition.

The key correction is equally important:

No disclaimer, code name, age gate, COA, creator separation, or account wall demonstrated a reliable safe harbor. FDA has already cited sellers using most of these configurations.

This is a high-demand, high-margin-looking, low-software-complexity market with visible churn. The durable assets are the audience, attribution, customer data, lifecycle system, creator network, logistics, quality records, and operating team—not any one investigational SKU.

The five funnel architectures

1. Open direct store

Search/social → public product → COA/reviews → discount ladder → cart → email/SMS/reorder

Examples: American Peptides, Modern Peptides, GL Peptides, Peptide Partners, Prime Peptides.

This architecture creates the least friction and the clearest price discovery. It is also where product copy, accessories, reviews, and use-adjacent language are easiest to observe together.

2. Qualified-access store

Search/public proof → account or research form → gated catalog → purchase → account lifecycle

Examples: Skye, Xcel, Loti, Ion, Mile High.

The better versions collect age, institution, business type, researcher type, intended context, or research protocol. The weaker versions ask only for email/password while calling the result “researcher access.”

3. Creator-to-coded-product funnel

Human-interest creator content → guide/community → tracked link/code → coded RUO product

Examples: Connor → V&L, Clavicular → Arcane, Zay → GL/Gentleman, Jesse → Peptira.

This is commercially powerful because the creator explains what the product name means and why the audience cares. The seller can keep first-party copy more technical. Public evidence still connects the two layers when the guide, code, and product route are explicit.

4. Creator-personalized storefront

Podcast/content → tracked link → creator-specific vendor page → curated categories → code → VIP/email

Best example: Sarah Westall → Limitless.

This is the strongest publicly substantiated conventional affiliate implementation in the study. The seller publishes its standard rates, the creator has a personalized page, and multiple tracking values and a buyer code are visible.

5. Owned-media and owned-company funnel

Founder media → free tool/course/podcast → seller → membership/course/email → repeated launches

Best example: Jay Campbell → BioLongevity.

Jay discloses that he is a cofounder, so this is not an ordinary affiliate relationship. It is the most durable media architecture in the sample, although no current BioLongevity retatrutide SKU was visible at the cutoff.

The controls that look most mature

Best purchaser qualification: Skye, Loti, PekCura, Ion

  • Skye has the strongest membership conduct language and expressly bars dosing/administration discussion.
  • Loti collects business type, EIN, and optional research identifiers.
  • PekCura exposed the deepest visible checkout questionnaire: date of birth, institution, entity, protocol, and two acknowledgments.
  • Ion collects identity, date of birth, institution, business type, and a separate marketing opt-in.

None of these public forms proves actual review or a legally accepted transaction.

Best affiliate governance: Ion

Ion’s public agreement requires material-connection disclosure and bars medical, fat-loss, dosing, administration, human-use, and similar claims. That is the clearest written creator-governance system in the sample. The next diligence question is whether it runs:

  • creator approval;
  • content pre-review;
  • automated monitoring;
  • evidence retention;
  • correction deadlines;
  • commission holds;
  • termination and code deactivation.

The public policy alone cannot answer those questions.

Best public QA architecture: Skye, Modern, Peptide Partners, PekCura

The strongest libraries map products to lots and expose more than one assay type. Peptide Partners and Skye show broad panels; Modern has a deep current/previous archive; PekCura has an unusually strong mass/purity/sterility/endotoxin example.

The Polaris case is the warning against overconfidence: three samples under one batch identifier produced radically different public results, while a later sample under the same batch name passed. The cause is unresolved, but the lesson is clear—one passing certificate does not prove batch uniformity.

Best retention engines: American, Modern, Loti, Gentleman

  • American: subscriptions, volume discounts, reviews, email/SMS, partner commissions, and accessories.
  • Modern: points worth roughly 5% of spend, email/SMS, account, broad strengths.
  • Loti: high-frequency SEO, newsletter, SMS offers, quantity pricing, and wholesale.
  • Gentleman: account, ebook nurture, 5% cashback, fast shipping, and creator attribution.

Best creator systems: Connor, Sarah, Jay

  • Connor owns education, community, recurring membership, and source translation.
  • Sarah has the strongest conventional tracked affiliate implementation.
  • Jay owns the deepest media/course/email stack and discloses company ownership.

The central marketing contradiction

The market repeatedly splits the message:

Store side Creator / content side
Research use only Personal result or transformation
Qualified researcher Mainstream fitness or wellness audience
Coded molecule Exact ingredient decoded
No administration guidance Guide, course, podcast, or practical use discussion
No therapeutic claim Weight, appetite, metabolism, body-composition framing
No human use Buyer code and direct seller handoff

This split converts because the creator answers the buyer’s real questions while the store closes the transaction. It also leaves a coherent public evidence trail when the creator names the product, seller, code, and practical use context.

Why the funnel converts

  1. The creator replaces institutional trust. Buyers trust a person, community, or transformation story before they trust an unknown chemical seller.
  2. COAs make the product feel legible. A third-party report converts uncertainty into a numerical purity story, even though it cannot prove the entire supply chain.
  3. Price ladders turn doubt into a deal. Discounts, packs, shipping thresholds, and limited offers make a high-risk category feel like ordinary ecommerce.
  4. Accessories eliminate friction. The store sells the surrounding workflow, not just a vial.
  5. Owned communities provide post-purchase support. Guides and calls reduce confusion and create recurring creator revenue.
  6. Attribution is simple. A link and code are enough to recruit creators quickly.
  7. Organic acquisition can make apparent CAC extremely low. Word of mouth and creator distribution allow the owner to capture the markup.
  8. The customer can reorder. The same catalog, account, email, and creator relationship support repeat transactions.

Why the market looks easier than it is

The storefront technology is ordinary—10 of 12 deeply audited stores used WordPress/WooCommerce. The hard parts are hidden:

  • reliable product provenance and lot-to-customer traceability;
  • payment settlement, reserves, disputes, and processor continuity;
  • inventory, shrinkage, shipping errors, and customer complaints;
  • creator screening and claims oversight;
  • accurate margin after discounts, refunds, shipping, commissions, and reserves;
  • legal-entity and contract continuity through domain changes;
  • quality systems that test more than one selected vial;
  • making a hot customer list valuable in a durable, lawful business.

Market-duration finding

The data does not support a precise “four to six months left” claim.

What it supports:

  • demand around obesity, metabolism, and longevity is durable;
  • retatrutide-specific gray-market demand may remain large before legitimate availability broadens;
  • scrutiny is already active rather than hypothetical;
  • agency action is uneven and can lag visible commerce;
  • warned sellers split among continued public sale, gated catalogs, product removal, domain migration, and closure;
  • many current sellers are genuinely new 2025–2026 entrants replacing firms that left or changed.

This behaves more like a churn-and-replacement market than a protected class of long-lived winners.

The strongest true continuity found was Particle Peptides, with the same Slovak company number on a 2015 archived storefront and the current site. Among U.S.-facing sellers, Loti, Battle Born, Xcel, Skye, Prime, PekCura, Hydro, Modern Aminos, Evolve, and Atomix have meaningful same-domain histories. Core .st and Limitless .st should not be presented as clean migrations because their older .com sites remained live with different contact details at the cutoff.

What this means for Braden

The full-funnel research strengthens the operating-partner thesis:

Braden may have a powerful transaction engine, but the visible market leaders show how much company infrastructure can still be built around it.

The most valuable systems Alex can bring are:

  1. Financial truth: order-to-settlement reconciliation, contribution margin, reserves, refunds, discounts, and affiliate payouts.
  2. Customer truth: first order, repeat order, cohort, product, code, source, and support burden.
  3. Affiliate operating system: creator CRM, screening, contracts, codes, links, content rules, approval, monitoring, payout, and termination.
  4. Lifecycle: welcome, cart recovery, post-purchase support, reorder timing, inventory alerts, win-back, and referral.
  5. Merchandising: product ladder, bundles, shipping threshold, subscriptions or points, and disciplined promotion.
  6. QA records: source, batch, report, inventory receipt, customer lot, complaint, and resolution.
  7. Fulfillment: remove Braden from daily packing; set SLAs, inventory controls, error tracking, and support escalation.
  8. Owned audience: media, community, education, experiences, and creator relationships that can survive a SKU or regulatory transition.
  9. Governance: accurate terms, consistent ages/policies, creator disclosure rules, evidence retention, and claims review.
  10. Buyer readiness: clean entity records, contracts, cohort data, quality records, management, and a financial model a buyer can diligence.

The biggest strategic asset Alex should negotiate around is not “helping with marketing.” It is converting accidental demand into:

  • owned first-party distribution;
  • documented repeat economics;
  • a managed creator network;
  • a business that does not require Braden to pack orders;
  • durable media, software, community, and operational infrastructure.

The numbers to verify before economics

Public research cannot validate Braden’s $1.5 million last-30-day claim, $90,000 day, or 80% margin. Before equity or a revenue share is priced, obtain:

  • every order and line item for at least 90 days;
  • gateway settlements and bank deposits;
  • refunds, chargebacks, reserves, holds, taxes, and processor fees;
  • discount and affiliate payout by order;
  • COGS, inbound freight, testing, packaging, shipping, and labor by batch;
  • inventory received, shipped, remaining, damaged, and replaced;
  • new versus repeat customers;
  • revenue and contribution by creator, code, product, and cohort;
  • every owner, equity holder, contractor, agency, web team, and decision right;
  • source, manufacturer, importer, lot, report, complaint, and customer mapping;
  • every regulator, processor, bank, insurer, or legal communication.

Bottom line

The winning commercial system is:

trusted attention → education → attributed product handoff → proof → offer → transaction → retention → advocacy

The best sellers have built several layers of that system. Braden appears, based on the call and public Biologix surface, to have captured the transaction before building the rest.

That is why the opportunity is real. It is also why Alex should negotiate as the operating principal who creates the company—not as a marketer paid only for posts or top-line revenue.

The research does not show that the active sellers discovered a magic disclaimer. It shows that demand is strong, ecommerce implementation is ordinary, enforcement is lagged and uneven, and the market continually replaces operators. The durable prize is ownership of the customer, distribution, data, community, operating system, and transition path.
