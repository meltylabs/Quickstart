02 / Monthly owner cash

Excess Cash pays out monthly: 70% Braden, 30% Alex.

Strategic inference: Proposed term — not agreed

Under the proposed terms, the company holds back only what the signed formula defines. Everything above those holdbacks is Excess Cash, and 100% of it is distributed at the monthly close — by rule, not discretion. Anything held back beyond the formula requires Alex's written approval.

The proposed monthly formula
  • Start with Cleared company cash at the monthly close
  • −minus Refunds and chargebacks not yet debited
  • −minus Accrued taxes, payroll, and accounts payable
  • −minus Signed inventory commitments due before the next close and processor obligations not already excluded
  • −minus Approved operating and legal-defense reserve
  • =equals Excess Cash — 100% distributed: 70% Braden · 30% Alex

03 / Required Reserve and owner rules

The reserve is a formula. Reinvestment is an election.

The Required Reserve is defined in a signed schedule, not by discretion. After distribution, either owner may lend cash back — but a loan never becomes ownership, priority, or a padded reserve.

Strategic inference: Proposed term

What may be held back — not yet agreed

  • Accrued taxes, documented refund or chargeback exposure, payroll, and incurred accounts payable.
  • Inventory counts only when an approved, executed commitment is payable before the next close; an optional growth order does not count.
  • Processor reserves or obligations count only when they are documented and not already excluded from Cleared Cash.
  • The operating reserve is capped at sixty days of the mutually approved budget.
  • The legal/compliance reserve is a fixed dollar amount in the signed schedule; capex requires both owners' written approval.
  • A dispute holds only the disputed amount. Undisputed Excess Cash still pays on time.
Strategic inference: Proposed term

Reinvestment is an owner election

  • First pay the full $490,000 / $210,000 distribution. Alex keeps his $210,000 and is never required to put it back.
  • Example: Braden may wire $300,000 of his own $490,000 back under a separate shareholder note, booked as a company loan payable to Braden.
  • The note is mutually approved, unsecured, non-convertible, and carries no warrants, votes, collateral, or ownership change.
  • Its principal, interest, and repayment priority cannot pad the Required Reserve or jump ahead of the monthly waterfall without Alex's written approval.
Strategic inference: Operating design

Finance control, openly installed

  • Braden's mother remains a trusted finance participant in the monthly close.
  • Alex installs the cash forecast, processor reconciliation, management pack, and independent controller cadence.
  • Alex receives read-only bank, processor, and accounting visibility plus narrow approval rights over reserve changes, related-party transfers, and owner benefits.
  • Personal draws or company-funded owner benefits sit in the same 70/30 equalization ledger.

08 / Activation gate

Nothing activates until the source records clear.

The economic right can be documented at signing. Authority, the public role, and live growth activate only when every gate passes.

  1. At least 95% of 30/90-day order value reconciles from source through processor and bank, with every residual difference explained.
  2. Fully loaded collected operating contribution is source-verified at 35% or better.
  3. Entity, cap table, side promises, related parties, vendor rights, IP, and customer data are fully disclosed and usable.
  4. Demand durability, concentration, refund/dispute rates, and repeat economics support the forecast.
  5. Qualified specialists support a lawful path, and quality evidence supports the contemplated scope.
  6. Braden accepts the role split, information rights, budget, decision rights, and management cadence in writing.

Failing a gate is not “walk forever.” It means no permanent title, voting control, personal guarantee, or broad Operating Partner liability — and any narrow paid remediation has explicit limits.
