# OVO Brand Outbound ICP

## Objective

Build a repeatable outbound engine for brands that:

1. Can spend meaningfully on creator acquisition.
2. Have a product creators can demonstrate or credibly discuss.
3. Are small enough that a decision-maker is reachable.
4. Can become recurring accounts rather than one-off posts.
5. Can operate within OVO's legal, platform, audience-safety, and reputation rules.

The initial goal is not to collect the largest possible account list. It is to create a short list of companies with both high commercial value and a high probability of replying.

## Proven thesis

Mealo, Bend Health, and Fitia establish the initial pattern:

- Consumer-facing product.
- Health, fitness, nutrition, or wellness use case.
- Customer acquisition matters economically.
- Creator content explains the product better than conventional advertising.
- The brand needs sourcing, coordination, execution, and accountability.
- OVO can sell and fulfill the work profitably.

OVO should preserve this wedge while expanding into regulated and consumer-app verticals with similarly large acquisition budgets.

## Master ICP

### Ideal company

| Attribute | Primary target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Company type | Consumer app, subscription product, ecommerce replenishment product, or regulated consumer platform | Repeat customer value supports recurring acquisition spend |
| Employee count | 20-200 | Best balance of budget and accessible leadership |
| Secondary size band | 201-500 | Larger budgets, but slower procurement and lower reply rates |
| Early-stage exception | 5-19 employees only when recently funded or visibly spending | Leadership replies, but budget must be proven |
| Enterprise treatment | 500+ employees only with a warm path, active RFP, regional buyer, or strong trigger | Cold reply probability is usually low and procurement is slow |
| Stage | Series A-C, growth equity, or profitable bootstrapped company | Capital and pressure to grow coexist |
| Geography | United States first; Canada, UK, and Australia second | Strong creator-market and commercial fit |
| Acquisition maturity | Already running paid social, affiliate, UGC, or creator campaigns | OVO sells scale and execution rather than educating from zero |
| Initial budget | At least $25,000 deployable for a pilot | Supports meaningful OVO contribution and enough creator inventory to prove value |
| Recurring potential | At least $100,000 annual creator or acquisition potential | Avoids a pipeline dominated by one-off posts |
| Buyer accessibility | Two or more verified growth, creator, partnerships, or marketing contacts | Prevents single-threaded outreach |
| Preferred payment model | Brand pays creators directly and pays OVO a separate fee | Protects working capital and makes contribution visible |

### Ideal operating problem

The best prospect already believes in creators but has one or more of these problems:

- Creator sourcing is manual and inconsistent.
- The internal growth team needs more content than it can produce.
- Individual creator deals are scattered across inboxes and spreadsheets.
- Campaign execution consumes senior marketing time.
- The brand lacks reliable fitness, wellness, lifestyle, gaming, or app-install talent.
- Paid social needs a recurring supply of authentic creative.
- The company is entering a new geography or launching a new product.
- Existing influencer activity produces content but not a coordinated acquisition program.
- The brand needs one accountable operator across creators, contracts, deadlines, approvals, and reporting.

## Reachability: the high-reply sweet spot

Budget and reply probability are separate. OVO should prioritize challenger brands, not default to category leaders.

### Highest-reply account pattern

- 20-150 employees.
- Founder, CMO, VP Growth, or Head of Partnerships remains publicly visible.
- Recently funded, launched, expanded, or hired into growth.
- Runs creator-style ads but has no large dedicated influencer department.
- Uses multiple small creators rather than only celebrities.
- Has a clear US growth mandate.
- Buyer posts on LinkedIn, attends industry events, appears on podcasts, or publishes hiring updates.
- Contact has a direct work email and at least one second channel.

### Lower-reply account pattern

- Fortune 500 or category-dominant company with entrenched agencies.
- Generic `marketing@` address is the only contact.
- No recent growth trigger.
- No visible creator or paid-social activity.
- Procurement must begin before a marketing owner is involved.
- Brand uses only celebrity partnerships or major league sponsorships.

Large strategic accounts remain in the CRM, but they should not consume the same outbound capacity as reachable challengers.

## Vertical ICPs

### 1. Health, fitness, nutrition, and wellness apps

**Priority:** Core

**Products**

- Calorie and food tracking.
- AI nutrition and meal planning.
- Workout programming and coaching.
- Stretching, mobility, recovery, and sleep.
- Meditation, mental wellness, and habit formation.
- Women's health, fertility, hormonal health, and menopause.
- Metabolic health and healthy-aging platforms.
- Wearable companion apps and health dashboards.

**Ideal company profile**

- 20-250 employees.
- Subscription or high-LTV consumer model.
- Meaningful US user-acquisition objective.
- Active Meta, TikTok, YouTube, affiliate, or creator spend.
- Needs fitness, wellness, lifestyle, nutrition, or transformation-oriented creators.
- Can run a 90-day creator program rather than a single sponsored post.

**Primary buyers**

- VP or Head of Growth.
- CMO or VP Marketing.
- Head of Influencer or Creator Marketing.
- Director of Performance Marketing.
- Head of Partnerships.
- Founder at companies below 50 employees.

**Best triggers**

- New funding.
- January or summer acquisition push.
- New AI feature, subscription tier, or US launch.
- Hiring for creator, affiliate, or performance marketing.
- Competitor creator campaign.
- Rapid app-store growth followed by a need for more creative.

**OVO offer**

> A managed creator-acquisition system for consumer health companies: creator sourcing, matching, contracting, execution, content approval, reporting, and renewal through one accountable team.

**Disqualifiers**

- Pre-product or pre-retention app.
- Total campaign budget below $25,000.
- Product requires unsupported medical claims.
- Brand expects guaranteed downloads or revenue without adequate measurement access.

### 2. Telehealth and compliant peptide-related businesses

**Priority:** Regulated growth

**Products**

- Licensed telehealth platforms.
- Weight-management programs.
- Dermatology, hair-loss, sexual-health, and hormonal-health services.
- Legitimate longevity and diagnostic platforms.
- Properly licensed clinics or providers offering lawfully marketed therapies.
- Peptide-related businesses only when legal, medical, pharmacy, and advertising controls pass.

**Ideal company profile**

- 20-300 employees.
- Licensed in every targeted market.
- Has internal legal or medical-review capacity.
- Uses a legitimate patient-provider and pharmacy structure where required.
- Can substantiate every health claim.
- Carries appropriate insurance.
- Has enough LTV to support substantial acquisition spend.
- Accepts locked scripts, required disclosures, and pre-publication approval.

**Primary buyers**

- CMO.
- VP Growth or Patient Acquisition.
- Head of Partnerships.
- Director of Influencer Marketing.
- Director of Brand.
- A named legal, medical, or regulatory approver must join before activation.

**Best triggers**

- State or national expansion.
- New service line.
- Recent funding.
- New chief marketing or growth leader.
- Existing direct-response creative with weak creator diversity.
- Brand begins recruiting clinical educators or ambassadors.

**OVO offer**

> A compliance-controlled health creator program that combines credible talent, locked claims, documented approvals, execution, and campaign evidence.

**Automatic rejection or legal hold**

- “Research use only” product promoted for human use.
- Prescription access without a legitimate prescribing relationship.
- Unsupported injury-healing, fat-loss, muscle-growth, disease, safety, or efficacy claims.
- Creator improvisation about medical outcomes.
- Before-and-after claims without approved substantiation.
- Brand asks OVO or creators to hide sponsorship or product status.
- No named legal or medical approver.
- Any product, jurisdiction, pharmacy, or claim that counsel cannot clear.

### 3. Sports betting, fantasy, and iGaming

**Priority:** Regulated growth

**Products**

- Licensed sportsbooks.
- Licensed iGaming operators.
- Daily fantasy and pick'em platforms where lawful.
- Sports-data, fan-engagement, and gaming-adjacent products with a legal operating model.

**Ideal company profile**

- 30-500 employees.
- Licensed in each targeted jurisdiction.
- Challenger, regional, or expanding operator with a reachable growth team.
- At least $100,000 in quarterly customer-acquisition capacity.
- Existing affiliate, creator, sports-media, or partnership program.
- Can provide state-level eligibility, age restrictions, responsible-gaming language, and compliance approval.

**Primary buyers**

- Head of Affiliates.
- Head of Creator or Influencer Marketing.
- VP Growth.
- Sports Marketing Director.
- Head of Partnerships.
- Regional Market Manager.
- Compliance approver is mandatory before launch.

**Best triggers**

- Entry into a newly available jurisdiction.
- Major sports season or event.
- New product, market-access agreement, or funding.
- Hiring affiliate or creator personnel.
- Challenger attempting to win share from an incumbent.
- Existing sports sponsorship without a scalable creator layer.

**OVO offer**

> A 21+ sports-creator acquisition program with verified adult audiences, jurisdiction controls, responsible-gaming review, documented approvals, and accountable execution.

**Mandatory controls**

- No Academy student, minor, or youth-facing creator participates.
- Creator and audience age fit must be evidenced.
- Jurisdiction and platform eligibility must be recorded before assignment.
- No guaranteed-winning, risk-free, or misleading financial language.
- Sponsorship and responsible-gaming disclosures are required.
- Campaigns use separate contracts, approval gates, and CRM risk labels.
- OVO will not take compensation tied to customer losses.

**Disqualifiers**

- Unlicensed or ambiguously licensed operator.
- Crypto casino targeting restricted markets.
- Brand requests evasion of age, geography, platform, or disclosure rules.
- Product primarily appeals to minors.
- No compliance function or documented campaign approval.

## Account qualification system

Every account receives three separate decisions. A high commercial score never overrides a failed compliance gate.

### A. Commercial fit score: 100 points

| Dimension | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Vertical and product fit | 15 | Product belongs to a priority vertical and creators can explain it naturally |
| Paid-acquisition maturity | 15 | Active paid-social, affiliate, creator, or UGC spending is visible |
| Budget capacity | 15 | Can support a $25K+ pilot or $100K+ annual program |
| Roster/audience fit | 15 | OVO has or can recruit credible creators for the target audience |
| Recurring economics | 10 | Subscription, repeat purchase, or strong customer LTV |
| Creator operating pain | 10 | Clear sourcing, volume, execution, creative, or coordination need |
| Timing trigger | 10 | Funding, launch, expansion, hiring, season, or competitive event |
| Buyer identified | 5 | Correct decision-maker and one additional stakeholder found |
| Payment structure | 5 | Brand can pay creators directly and OVO separately |

**Commercial thresholds**

- 85-100: Named-account priority.
- 70-84: Qualified outbound.
- 55-69: Nurture or enrich.
- Below 55: Do not work.

### B. Reply-likelihood score: 50 points

| Signal | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Reachable company size | 10 | 20-150 employees or a reachable title-level business unit |
| Recent trigger | 10 | Material event within 90 days |
| Visible buyer | 10 | Buyer publishes, posts, speaks, hires, or accepts direct contact |
| Existing creator behavior | 10 | Small/mid-tier creators or UGC are already in use |
| Contactability | 5 | Verified email plus LinkedIn, phone, event, or partner route |
| Organizational gap | 5 | Creator need is visible but no mature internal creator team exists |

**Reply thresholds**

- 40-50: High-touch sequence and call block now.
- 30-39: Standard multichannel outbound.
- 20-29: Enrich or wait for a trigger.
- Below 20: Do not spend human sales time.

### C. Compliance and reputation gate

| State | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Ordinary consumer product with standard advertising obligations | Standard commercial review |
| Yellow | Health, supplements, education involving minors, finance, dating safety, gaming monetization, or another material claim/risk | Specialist checklist and approval before proposal |
| Red | Unlicensed gambling, evasive geography, undisclosed sponsorship, unsupported medical/financial claims, gray-market human-use peptide promotion, deceptive product, or unwillingness to provide evidence | Reject or place on legal hold |

An account becomes outbound-ready only when:

- Commercial fit is at least 70.
- Reply likelihood is at least 30.
- Compliance is green or yellow with a named approval path.
- At least two contacts are identified.
- A specific reason to contact the company now is recorded.

## Account tiers and sales treatment

### Tier A: reachable challengers

- Commercial score 85+.
- Reply score 40+.
- Usually 20-150 employees.
- Founder or senior buyer is reachable.
- Strong trigger and visible creator behavior.

Treatment: research-driven email, LinkedIn touch, direct call, creator or partner path, and founder involvement where it materially improves the close.

### Tier B: qualified scale-ups

- Commercial score 70+.
- Reply score 30+.
- Usually 100-500 employees.
- Multiple stakeholders and a longer buying process.

Treatment: coordinated multichannel sequence across three to five contacts, account-level messaging, and trigger-based follow-up.

### Tier C: strategic enterprise

- Large budget but low cold accessibility.
- Usually 500+ employees or category leaders with incumbent agencies.

Treatment: introductions, partners, conferences, creator referrals, regional teams, RFP monitoring, and executive relationship development. Do not let these accounts dominate weekly activity metrics.

## Required account and contact fields

No company enters an active sequence without:

- Legal and trading name.
- Website and primary geography.
- Vertical and subvertical.
- Employee band and funding/revenue evidence.
- Product model and estimated customer economics.
- Paid-social, creator, affiliate, or UGC evidence.
- Recent trigger with source and date.
- Primary buyer, secondary buyer, and compliance approver when required.
- Verified email and at least one secondary contact path.
- Commercial score.
- Reply-likelihood score.
- Compliance state and reason.
- OVO roster-fit hypothesis.
- Personalized problem hypothesis.
- Recommended offer.
- Owner, next action, due date, and suppression state.

| 50-200 employees | VP Growth, CMO, or Head of Creator | Performance, partnerships, brand |
| 200-500 employees | Head of Influencer, Director Growth, or BU marketing lead | VP Marketing, procurement, partnerships |

## Outbound positioning

OVO is not selling access to a spreadsheet of influencers. It is selling an accountable creator operating system.

### Core positioning

> OVO builds and operates creator acquisition programs for consumer brands. We source the right creators, manage contracting and delivery, control approvals, preserve campaign evidence, and give the brand one accountable team from brief through renewal.

### Proof

- Bend Health: approximately $36,000 in campaign-level contribution to OVO, with creators paid directly by the brand.
- Mealo: management-reported approximately $30,000 in contribution.
- Fitia: approximately $40,753 in reconciled contribution after creator payouts, payment fees, and commission.
- Combined proof: approximately $106,753 in campaign contribution across three inbound relationships.

These figures are internal sales proof. External use requires approved language, client permission, and reconciled supporting evidence.

## Standard campaign shape

The preferred initial motion is a 90-day program:

- Defined audience and creator profile.
- Sourcing and qualification.
- Contract and rights coordination.
- Creator communication and deadline management.
- Compliance and brand approvals.
- Content delivery and evidence.
- Performance reporting.
- Renewal and expansion recommendation.

Preferred economics:

1. Brand pays creators directly.
2. Brand pays OVO a separate strategy and management fee.
3. New or one-off brands pre-fund any OVO-controlled creator exposure.
4. Every proposal shows expected collected cash, direct costs, variable commission, labor, payment risk, and OVO contribution.

## Routing and approval

1. RevOps verifies identity, contactability, scores, source, and suppression.
2. Brand SDR owns first contact and qualification until a real opportunity exists.
3. The assigned Closing AE owns discovery, proposal, negotiation, and close.
4. Jaci or the campaign-operations owner validates creator capacity and deliverability before proposal.
5. Patricia accepts the post-close brand relationship and account plan.
6. Yellow compliance accounts require a named specialist approval path before proposal.
7. Gambling, telehealth, peptide, finance, and youth-related campaigns cannot advance to fulfillment without documented legal/compliance approval.
8. Red accounts are rejected; revenue potential does not create an exception.

## Outbound operating targets

The first outbound build should prioritize quality and learning:

- 100 Tier A accounts per quarter.
- Three verified contacts per account.
- No account activated without a reason-now trigger.
- Work the top 25 accounts each week through coordinated email, LinkedIn, calls, creator introductions, and partner routes.
- Measure positive reply rate, qualified-meeting rate, opportunity rate, proposal rate, win rate, collected cash, contribution, and days to close by vertical.
- Reallocate capacity monthly toward verticals producing qualified replies and collected contribution, not opens or raw meetings.

Initial reply target is not a universal benchmark. Establish OVO's baseline after the first 300 fully qualified contacts, segmented by vertical, score band, persona, channel, and trigger.

## Stop rules

- Do not sequence an account simply because the category has money.
- Do not let enterprise logos crowd out reachable challengers.
- Do not call gifting, product seeding, or an unpaid ambassador program a qualified opportunity.
- Do not advance interest without budget, authority, need, timing, and a next step.
- Do not mix Academy students or youth-facing creators into gambling campaigns.
- Do not promote a peptide, drug, supplement, financial product, or gambling product without the required evidence and approval.
- Do not allow pass-through billings to hide low contribution.
- Do not accept creator-payment float from a new or one-off client without pre-funding.
- Do not scale a vertical because it generates replies; scale it when replies become collected contribution without unacceptable risk.

## Thirty-day launch sequence

### Week 1: data and proof

- Configure vertical, score, trigger, buyer, compliance, and suppression fields in the CRM.
- Approve internal proof language for Bend, Mealo, and Fitia.
- Build the first 25 Tier A health/fitness accounts.
- Build 10 each for telehealth/peptides, gambling, mobile gaming, dating, education, and broader subscriptions.

### Week 2: controlled activation

- Activate only accounts meeting both score thresholds.
- Run one message thesis per vertical.
- Contact three stakeholders per account through coordinated channels.
- Require same-day logging of every reply, call, objection, and next action.

### Week 3: qualification learning

- Review positive and negative replies by vertical, trigger, company size, and persona.
- Remove segments producing curiosity but no budgets.
- Improve account selection before rewriting every message.
- Route regulated interest through its compliance path before commercial commitments.

### Week 4: allocation decision

- Rank verticals by positive replies, qualified meetings, expected contribution, sales-cycle speed, fulfillment fit, and compliance burden.
- Double capacity only on verticals producing qualified opportunities.
- Preserve health/fitness as the control group because it has closed-won proof.
