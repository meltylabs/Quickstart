# OVO Labs — Competitor Website Audit

**Audit date:** July 24, 2026

**Scope:** Public desktop/mobile structure, discovery, collections, PDPs, testing/document presentation, cart/lifecycle mechanics, performance signals, and claim risk.

## Bottom line

American Peptides is the strongest public merchandising reference in the direct set. Modern Peptides is the strongest public evidence-state reference. Neither is a complete model for OVO Labs.

OVO Labs’ better synthesis is:

`American-style product finding + Modern-style state clarity + scientific-supplier taxonomy + ecommerce-tested mobile density + zero fabricated proof`

No reviewed company exposes normalized conversion data. “Highest converting” is unknowable.

## Scoring method

Each dimension is scored from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong) based only on publicly observable interface execution.

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Search and navigation | 20% |
| Collection and product finding | 20% |
| PDP decision clarity | 20% |
| Testing/document state | 20% |
| Mobile and performance discipline | 10% |
| Coherence and claim restraint | 10% |

Scores do not evaluate material quality, legal status, real conversion, fulfillment, or underlying evidence validity.

## 1. American Peptides

### Observable strengths

- Full primary navigation across Shop, Bundles, COA, Learn, account, wholesale, and partner paths.
- Category navigation with counts and broad coverage.
- Prominent search and cart.
- Product grids expose names, prices, formats, and Add controls.
- Homepage moves quickly from hero into catalog.
- Bundles, product records, education, FAQs, and service statements cover the full buying journey.
- Testing/report links appear close to product decisions.

### Observable weaknesses

- Very high claim and promotional density.
- Many competing navigation paths.
- Trust modules, product grids, educational blocks, and lifecycle offers create a long page.
- Review, purity, testing, domestic-sourcing, shipping, and customer-volume claims require proof outside the interface.
- Adjacent preparation supplies and body-effect language increase intended-use risk.

### Transfer to OVO Labs

- Exposed search.
- Clear category architecture.
- Early product grid.
- Full card anatomy.
- Product-adjacent testing link.
- Bundles with transparent included items.
- Complete footer route map.

### Reject

- Claims waterfall.
- Use-adjacent supplies.
- outcome-coded bundle names;
- fake or unsupported proof;
- promotional density;
- affiliate/lifestyle mechanics.

**Evidence:** [current public site](https://shop.americanpeptides.us/) — Grade A for visible interface.

## 2. Modern Peptides

### Observable strengths

- Clean contemporary storefront structure.
- Public COA center.
- Current, upcoming, and historical document states create a useful mental model.
- Account and points systems make retention visible.
- Product/document relationships are easier to inspect than on many competitors.

### Observable weaknesses

- Commerce and loyalty can compete with document hierarchy.
- A public document state still does not prove chain of custody or underlying validity.
- More differentiation is needed between available, pending, superseded, and unrelated records.

### Transfer to OVO Labs

- Explicit record-state vocabulary.
- Searchable product/document relationship.
- Testing center as a top-level route.
- Identity match even when no result exists.

### Reject

- Points or replenishment during the concept stage.
- Any visual treatment that makes pending or historical evidence look current.

**Evidence:** [homepage](https://modern-peptides.com/), [COA library](https://modern-peptides.com/coa/) — Grade A.

## 3. Protide Health

### Observable strengths

- Verification and testing are prominent.
- Account, order, support, and fulfillment confidence are easy to find.
- Official-channel and authenticity warnings address a real category concern.
- Compact trust architecture supports the purchase path.

### Observable weaknesses

- Repeated trust claims can become wallpaper.
- Some useful detail is split across public and account surfaces.
- Fulfillment assertions are easy to copy visually but impossible for OVO Labs to claim.

### Transfer to OVO Labs

- Testing & COAs in primary navigation.
- Clear canonical-domain and anti-impersonation guidance if needed.
- Compact help architecture.

### Reject

- Shipping or support promises without measured operating records.

**Evidence:** [current public site](https://protidehealth.com/) — Grade A.

## 4. Pure U.S. Peptides

### Observable strengths

- PDPs behave like educational pages.
- Literature, research context, and related products deepen exploration.
- Technical content improves organic-search surface.
- Product identity remains visible through long-form content.

### Observable weaknesses

- Long pages increase scanning cost.
- Literature about a molecule can be mistaken for evidence about the listed material.
- Product, education, and purchase action need stronger boundaries.

### Transfer to OVO Labs

- Source-aware Learn content.
- Related-product exploration.
- Neutral product-record fields.
- Clear article-to-product internal linking.

### Reject

- Using scientific literature to imply a specific product result or human outcome.
- Unbounded PDP length before core decision information.

**Evidence:** [current public site](https://pureuspeptide.com/) — Grade A.

## 5. Loti Labs

### Observable strengths

- Broad catalog.
- Search, order utilities, resources, and public COA library.
- Large content footprint.
- Many routes capture long-tail demand.

### Observable weaknesses

- Crowded navigation and high page density.
- Mixed product types complicate taxonomy.
- Discount and promotional layers can weaken scientific credibility.
- Broad catalog makes consistent product-record and testing presentation harder.

### Transfer to OVO Labs

- Search synonyms.
- Clear category filters.
- Useful zero-result recovery.
- Content taxonomy and internal linking.

### Reject

- Breadth before data.
- Promotion-led hierarchy.
- Mixing supplies and product concepts.

**Evidence:** [current public site](https://lotilabs.com/), [COA library](https://lotilabs.com/coas-updated/) — Grade A.

## 6. Core Peptides

### Observable strengths

- Broad, familiar collection architecture.
- Search-oriented molecule coverage.
- Product naming aligns with common search behavior.
- Catalog breadth supports acquisition.

### Observable weaknesses

- Breadth creates comparison and document-matching burden.
- Common ecommerce modules can make technical products feel interchangeable.
- Public crawlability was intermittent during review, which limits complete comparison.

### Transfer to OVO Labs

- Common names and abbreviations in search.
- Flat, recognizable categories.

### Reject

- Mass page creation before editorial and evidence operations.

**Evidence:** [current public site](https://www.corepeptides.com/) — Grade A where observable; incomplete crawl.

## 7. Skye Peptides

### Observable strengths

- Visible account-first posture.
- Access control communicates selectivity.
- Deeper report/catalog access appears gated.

### Observable weaknesses

- A hard gate prevents evaluation before commitment.
- Hidden public evidence increases uncertainty.
- Product finding cannot demonstrate value to anonymous visitors.

### Transfer to OVO Labs

- If a future approved model requires qualification, gate the transaction—not the basic product and evidence-policy explanation.

### Reject

- Login wall around all useful information.
- Checkbox-only qualification.

**Evidence:** [public login](https://skyepeptides.com/login/) — Grade A for visible gate; actual screening rigor unknown.

## Adjacent scientific-supplier audit

### What these companies do better

- Predictable global search.
- Scientific identity first.
- Stable product codes.
- Clear tabs or sections for specifications, documents, references, and safety.
- Distinct custom-project intake.
- Document retrieval as a primary job.
- Less dependence on lifestyle merchandising.

### What OVO Labs should borrow

| Pattern | OVO Labs implementation |
|---|---|
| Stable product code | OVO-001 through OVO-010 |
| Stable concept-set code | OVO-S01 through OVO-S03 |
| Scientific taxonomy | Four neutral categories |
| Predictable record anatomy | Same PDP sequence |
| Document retrieval | Search name/code, then show explicit state |
| Custom services separation | Not built until approved |
| Related references | Learn articles clearly separated from product evidence |

## Cross-site journey audit

### Homepage

The strongest direct sites make the catalog visible quickly. OVO Labs should establish its warm retail-house identity without delaying the first product row beyond the first 1000px desktop viewport.

### Search

Technical catalogs serve many directed visitors. Search should be an input, not an icon, and support names, codes, categories, and common abbreviations.

### Category

Broad walls of products raise choice cost. OVO Labs should use four clear categories, visual entry tiles, useful filters, clear applied state, and stable sorting.

### Product card

Most stores force a tradeoff between minimal cards and claim-heavy cards. OVO Labs’ card should be complete but neutral: identity, format, descriptor, testing state, price, Add.

### PDP

The decision block should answer what the concept is, its format, concept price, current testing state, and next action before long explanation. Supporting content belongs in compact accordions.

### Testing

A document library is only useful when a visitor can identify the exact product, state, scope, and date. OVO Labs’ current honest answer is “No result reported.”

### Cart

A dense upsell cart is unnecessary. OVO Labs needs quantity, remove, subtotal, clear, and an unmistakable end-of-demo state.

## CRO evidence applied

### Search visibility

Blend Commerce reports an Oaktree test in which exposed mobile search improved conversion and revenue per visitor. Because this is a single client case study, OVO Labs treats it as a strong hypothesis, not a guarantee. Search remains exposed and instrumented.

### Category navigation

The same Oaktree program reports a large mobile conversion lift for visual subcategory discovery variants. OVO Labs uses visual categories and will compare their engagement with text navigation.

### Mobile product density

Gym King reports deliberately fitting two full products in a mobile viewport among its useful tests. OVO Labs adopts this as a design constraint and validates readability and mis-tap rates.

### Ratings

Gym King reports that adding ratings where only 60–70% of products had reviews reduced revenue per visitor by 10%. OVO Labs shows no ratings until verified coverage exists.

### PDP disclosures

Oaktree reports revenue-per-visitor improvement from expanded accordion navigation. OVO Labs uses accordions below a complete decision block, not as a place to hide critical information.

### Speed

The Nuvemshop case published on web.dev links above-fold image prioritization to major LCP health improvement and higher mobile organic conversion/cart engagement. OVO Labs prioritizes hero media and avoids above-fold lazy loading and transitions.

### Product lists

Baymard’s benchmark shows widespread product-list weakness, especially on mobile. OVO Labs therefore treats collection UX as a release-critical system, not a generic grid.

## Exact OVO Labs build decisions

### Ship in the fictional storefront

1. Thin fiction banner.
2. Sticky ecommerce header.
3. Persistent predictive search.
4. Category subnavigation.
5. Compact 440px hero.
6. First product row in the first desktop viewport.
7. Four-up desktop and two-up mobile product cards.
8. Complete PDP decision block.
9. Product-adjacent “No result reported.”
10. Searchable Testing & COAs page.
11. Transparent concept sets.
12. Functional local demo cart with no checkout.
13. Learn library with neutral source-aware content.
14. Performance and accessibility budgets.
15. Warm apothecary-modernist imagery restricted to safe amber-vial still lifes with no use-adjacent props or invented evidence.

### Do not ship

1. Reviews, stars, customer counts, or testimonials.
2. Lab names, logos, reports, purity, lots, or certificates.
3. Inventory, availability, scarcity, urgency, shipping, returns, or service claims.
4. Preparation tools or administration-adjacent products.
5. Human outcome, dosing, reconstitution, safety, or efficacy content.
6. Product, Offer, AggregateRating, stock, or shipping schema.
7. Checkout, payment, quote, preorder, reservation, or waitlist controls.
8. Dead search, filter, sort, newsletter, Add, or cart controls.

## QA questions for the next pass

### Five-second comprehension

- Can a first-time visitor name the brand and category?
- Do they immediately see how to shop concepts?
- Do they understand that the site is fictional?

### Product finding

- Can every product be found by exact name, code, category, and abbreviation?
- Is search visible without opening a menu?
- Does zero-result search recover gracefully?
- Can filters be combined and reset?

### Decision quality

- Is the testing state visible on the card and PDP?
- Does the first PDP screen contain every core decision field?
- Are accordions labeled by the question they answer?
- Are related concepts obviously distinct from bundles or protocols?

### Mobile

- Are two complete cards visible without unreadable copy?
- Are Add controls at least 44px and full-width?
- Do drawers trap and restore focus?
- Is there any horizontal overflow at 320px?

### Truth

- Is any number mistaken for a real test, lot, stock, customer, or result?
- Is any concept price mistaken for an offer?
- Does any interaction look like checkout?
- Is “No result reported” the default evidence state?

### Performance

- Is the hero image prioritized?
- Are below-fold images lazy-loaded with dimensions?
- Are fonts and scripts limited?
- Are LCP, INP, and CLS within budget?

## Evidence limitations

- Public websites reveal interface, not economics or compliance.
- Self-published metrics may be accurate but usually lack raw data.
- Agency case studies are selected winners.
- Forum posts can be anonymous, promotional, or manipulated.
- A visible certificate does not prove sample identity or chain of custody.
- A conversion pattern that worked in apparel or memorial products may fail for OVO Labs.

The audit produces hypotheses. OVO Labs’ own test results decide what stays.
