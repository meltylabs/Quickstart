# OVO Labs — End-to-End Marketing Plan

*Current scope: fictional storefront and concept validation. Every revenue tactic is conditional on all launch gates.*

## Executive strategy

OVO Labs should build a recognizable peptide retail house around a better way to find and evaluate product concepts—not a medical outcome and not an evidence claim it cannot prove.

The concept-learning journey is:

`high-intent discovery → fast search/category match → compact PDP decision block → explicit testing state → demo add → local cart → concept-learning loop`

During the concept phase, the final conversion is a measured interaction, not a transaction. There is no checkout, inventory, order, shipping, or sales follow-up.

There is also no external email program, paid acquisition, referral or affiliate program, advertising pixel, CRM sync, audience building, or commercial remarketing. The prototype records only browser-local usability events. Every acquisition or lifecycle system described below is a conditional design—not authorization to activate it.

## Positioning

### Category

Warm, product-led peptide ecommerce concept.

### Promise

**Peptide science, refined.**

The functional promise beneath it remains: find the compound, see the state, and decide without the noise.

### Core contrast

Most peptide stores maximize claims and merchandising density; many scientific catalogs maximize documentation density. OVO Labs combines warm editorial retail presentation, the product-finding speed of modern ecommerce, and the plain evidence boundaries of a careful research interface.

### Reasons to believe today

- Distinctive `ovolabs.` retail-house identity.
- Visible, predictive product search.
- Compact category and product navigation.
- Consistent PDP decision blocks.
- Testing status at the decision point.
- Fast static experience.
- Working local demo cart.
- No fabricated reviews, labs, purity, stock, shipping, or urgency.

These are observable product attributes, not operational proof.

### Brand expression

- Lead with `ovolabs.` and **Peptide science, refined.**
- Use warm apothecary-modernist composition to make a narrow catalog feel considered, not scarce.
- Keep product identity, format, concept price, and testing state easier to find than the art direction.
- Use amber-vial still lifes only as concept imagery; never as evidence of material, stock, packaging production, quality, or a laboratory.
- Never show syringes, preparation supplies, body context, fake reports, purity labels, or outcome-oriented packaging.

## Audiences

### Primary concept-testing audience

Ecommerce shoppers and research-material buyers who can assess search, collection, PDP, testing, and cart usability without assuming the site is an operating vendor.

### Secondary audience

Ecommerce operators, CRO specialists, designers, and research-procurement professionals evaluating the concept’s information architecture.

### Conditional future ICP

Only after written legal and operating approval: qualified organizations with a legitimate research-material need, documented purchasing authority, and the ability to satisfy the approved access model.

### Excluded audience and intent

- people seeking personal use;
- medical, treatment, dosing, administration, or preparation guidance;
- clinics or creators seeking unapproved consumer outcomes;
- resellers or affiliates centered on human outcomes;
- anyone attempting to bypass an approved access policy.

## Jobs to be done

1. “Take me to the compound I already know.”
2. “Help me scan the catalog without decoding the vendor’s taxonomy.”
3. “Tell me what evidence exists before I commit attention.”
4. “Let me compare product concepts and a set without losing context.”
5. “Explain a technical distinction in plain language.”

## Website conversion system

### Global header

- Thin fiction banner above everything.
- Sticky store header.
- `ovolabs.` wordmark.
- Exposed search input with predictive results.
- Primary links: Shop, Bundles, Testing & COAs, Learn.
- Cart count based only on browser-local demo state.
- Category subnavigation: Shop All, GLP-1 & Metabolic, Research Peptides, Growth Hormone Research, Blends.

### Homepage sequence

1. **Hero:** `ovolabs.` identity, “Peptide science, refined,” Shop concepts action, safe amber-vial still life, and product/testing preview.
2. **Trust strip:** interface truths only—fast search, honest testing states, local demo cart.
3. **Category tiles:** immediate self-selection.
4. **Product row:** first products visible within the first desktop viewport and near the first mobile scroll.
5. **Testing feature:** a document-like “No result reported” example.
6. **Concept sets:** transparent included items and arithmetic.
7. **Learn:** three concise guides tied to catalog decisions.
8. **Newsletter demo:** local success state; nothing transmitted and no email sent.
9. **Compact footer:** routes, fiction state, no operational claims.

### Collection page

- Visible result count and category title.
- Search within results.
- Multi-select category filters where useful.
- Clear applied-filter state.
- Sort by featured, name, and concept price.
- Four-up desktop grid.
- Two-up mobile grid with readable product names and full-width Add controls.
- No ratings, inventory, sale badges, popularity claims, or “best seller” labels.
- Zero-results state that preserves the search term and offers clear reset/category paths.

### Product card

Each card contains:

1. product image;
2. category;
3. name and concept format;
4. neutral one-line descriptor;
5. testing micro-row: “Testing: no result reported”;
6. concept price;
7. full-width Add control.

### PDP decision block

The first decision region must contain:

- breadcrumb;
- name and product code;
- category and concept format;
- neutral descriptor;
- concept price;
- testing state;
- Add control;
- explicit concept-price and no-sale context accessible without hunting.

Below it:

- testing and report-state explanation;
- concept specifications;
- expandable product-record fields;
- related concepts;
- no claims about use, outcomes, safety, or efficacy.

### Testing & COAs

- Search by product name or code.
- Return a product identity match even when evidence is absent.
- Default state: “No result reported.”
- Explain that no laboratory, method, sample, lot, report date, or result exists.
- Show the fields a future report would require without drawing a fake chromatogram or certificate.
- Never imply that a generic document supports a product.

### Demo cart

- Add, remove, quantity, subtotal, and clear all work.
- Cart persists locally.
- Cart state is announced accessibly.
- No checkout control.
- End state: “This is where the demo ends. Nothing is stocked, sold, or shippable.”

## Search and selective-indexing strategy

### Current concept

- Keep every canonical route `noindex, nofollow`.
- Do not submit the concept to search engines, publish an XML sitemap for discovery, buy search traffic, or treat organic impressions as a concept KPI.
- Maintain clear title, description, canonical, breadcrumb, and article metadata so direct links are understandable and the implementation is technically ready for review; metadata does not override the non-indexing directive.
- Keep Product and Offer schema absent.
- Keep one strong page per approved user intent; avoid mass-generated molecule pages.
- Do not target “buy,” dosing, reconstitution, body outcome, weight loss, or treatment queries.

### Selective-indexing gate

Indexing can begin only after all of the following are true:

1. legitimate operations, exact catalog, jurisdiction, access model, and intended-use controls pass the written launch gates;
2. counsel approves each candidate page, its claims, citations, metadata, structured data, internal links, and likely query neighborhood;
3. the claims owner maps every factual statement to current evidence and establishes a review/withdrawal process;
4. privacy, analytics, consent, security, and retention reviews are complete;
5. robots, canonical, sitemap, and removal procedures are tested in a non-production environment;
6. the first indexable cohort is deliberately narrow and begins with approved company, policy, and evidence-literacy pages—not automatically with product pages;
7. each later product or catalog page receives page-level approval before its robots directive changes.

The pilot should monitor index coverage, actual queries, unsupported-claim incidents, consumer or human-use intent, and qualified-organization actions. Any prohibited query pattern, misleading snippet, stale claim, or operating-gate regression pauses expansion and triggers correction or de-indexing.

Keyword or campaign activation requires a separate review of the exact query, landing page, copy, catalog, geography, audience, and platform policy. A “research use only” negative keyword or disclaimer is not a substitute for compliant intended use.

### Search KPIs

| Stage | KPI | Required state |
|---|---|---|
| Current concept | Indexed canonical routes | 0 |
| Current concept | External organic acquisition target | Disabled |
| Current concept | Internal product-search success and zero-result rate | Measured browser-locally |
| Conditional pilot | Approved pages indexed | Equals the counsel-approved allowlist only |
| Conditional pilot | Prohibited or human-use query incidents | 0; pause and review any incident |
| Conditional pilot | Unsupported or stale indexed claims | 0 |
| Conditional pilot | Qualified-organization action rate | Diagnostic only until volume and legitimacy are established |

## Lifecycle, email, and retention plan

### Current state: no external lifecycle

There is no commercial lifecycle today. The newsletter control is a local interaction demo: it transmits no address, sends no message, adds no contact to a list, and creates no sales follow-up. There is no ESP, CRM, customer database, email automation, referral system, affiliate platform, advertising pixel, or product-level remarketing audience.

### Concept visitor

1. Finds a product or guide.
2. Tests search or category navigation.
3. Reviews a PDP and testing state.
4. Uses the demo cart.
5. Returns through saved local state or a direct route.

### Conditional future lifecycle

Only after launch gates:

1. Evidence/education discovery.
2. Approved qualification flow.
3. Product and report review.
4. Authorized order process.
5. Record delivery and support.
6. Reorder and exception handling.

### Email activation gates

No external email collection or send is allowed until all of these are complete:

- written approval for the exact operating model, audience, jurisdiction, catalog, and email content;
- a published privacy notice and consent language that distinguish marketing from required transactional communication;
- data-flow, processor, security, access-control, retention, deletion, incident-response, and cross-border review;
- verified sender identity, SPF, DKIM, DMARC, reply handling, suppression handling, and unsubscribe testing;
- double opt-in for optional marketing, timestamped consent evidence, and a preference center;
- a claims owner and approval workflow for every subject line, preview, body, link, image, and landing page;
- separate transactional and marketing streams so an unsubscribe never blocks a required order, safety, quality, withdrawal, or recall notice;
- test records proving that no raw search term, product view, testing lookup, cart content, intended use, condition, protocol, personal health detail, or inferred health interest enters the ESP or CRM.

### Conditional email architecture

| Flow | Trigger and cadence | One job per message | Data allowed | Hard boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consent confirmation | Immediately after double opt-in | Confirm the requested subscription and preferences | Address, consent timestamp, source, jurisdiction, preference | No product or outcome persuasion |
| Education welcome | 3 messages over 7–10 days | Explain evidence-state vocabulary, catalog navigation, and publication standards | Subscription state and aggregate engagement | No human-use, dosing, preparation, efficacy, safety, or personal-health content |
| Evidence update | Monthly or event-based after claims review | Announce a materially updated guide, policy, or verified record state | Topic preference at a broad, non-health level | Never imply that literature supports a listed item or outcome |
| Preference check | After sustained inactivity; one reminder | Let the recipient reduce frequency or leave | Last-delivery date and subscription preference | No product-view or cart-triggered win-back |
| Sunset | After the approved inactivity period | Stop sending and retain only the suppression record | Minimal suppression data | No “last chance,” scarcity, or escalating offer |
| Qualification operations | Only after an organization starts an approved flow | Explain required next step or status | Organization, authorized contact, workflow status | Not a disguise for consumer marketing |
| Transactional operations | Only after legitimate commerce is approved and an authorized order exists | Receipt, record delivery, service, exception, withdrawal, or recall communication | Minimum order and contact data required to perform the duty | No cross-sell in safety, quality, withdrawal, or recall messages |

Product-level browse abandonment, cart recovery, replenishment, outcome segmentation, and inferred-interest campaigns are excluded. They create disproportionate privacy and intended-use risk and are not part of the initial future lifecycle.

### Email measurement and stop rules

Measure consent completion, confirmed delivery, bounce, unsubscribe, complaint, preference change, support reply, and approved workflow completion. Do not optimize on product interest, personal outcome, or health-intent proxies.

Pause the affected flow immediately if any of the following occurs:

- consent cannot be demonstrated;
- an unsubscribe or suppression fails;
- a privacy, security, or unintended-recipient incident occurs;
- prohibited content or an unsupported claim ships;
- product, cart, search, testing, condition, protocol, or inferred-health data reaches an email or advertising system;
- recipient complaints exceed the conservative threshold approved before launch;
- the audience or geography drifts beyond the approved scope.

Restart requires documented root cause, corrected records, regression testing, and approval from privacy, claims, and operating owners.

## Referral and affiliate plan

### Current state

There is no referral or affiliate program, unique referral link, reward, commission, ambassador tier, creator seeding, or attributed product referral. Concept testers may receive a manually shared, non-incentivized usability-study invitation; that is research recruitment, not product promotion.

### Conditional organizational referral pilot

Referral activation requires legitimate operations, a stable qualified-organization lifecycle, observed unit economics, approved terms, tax and accounting treatment, privacy review, anti-bribery and conflict review, claims training, and a tested fraud/reversal process.

The first pilot should be deliberately narrow:

1. invite only verified organizational customers or disclosed professional partners in the approved jurisdiction;
2. use a fixed, transparent, modest reward tied to an accepted qualified organization—not traffic, clicks, product interest, personal use, or an unverified order;
3. require clear disclosure of the relationship wherever a referral is made;
4. route every referred organization through the same independent qualification and evidence review;
5. delay any reward until the approved verification and reversal window closes;
6. prohibit self-referrals, duplicate entities, circular referrals, coupon publishing, bidding on brand terms, impersonation, and undisclosed sub-affiliates;
7. preserve reward reversal, suspension, audit, and termination rights;
8. expose the full referred-user experience and terms before collecting data.

Creators centered on consumer outcomes, personal health claims, body transformation, treatment, dosing, preparation, or coded human-use testimonials are categorically ineligible. No partner may make a claim OVO Labs could not publish itself. Referral parameters must not encode a molecule, condition, protocol, intended use, or other health-interest signal.

An affiliate program should not launch with the referral pilot. Consider it only after the narrower program produces clean qualification, attribution, fraud, complaint, and economics evidence over a predeclared review period.

### Referral measurement and stop rules

Measure verified referring organizations, referred-organization qualification completion, accepted referrals, duplicate/fraud flags, reversal rate, disclosure compliance, complaint rate, incremental acquisition cost, and referred-account quality. Do not count clicks, consumer product interest, or unqualified form fills as success.

Pause the program for any missing disclosure, prohibited claim, consumer or personal-use demand, qualification bypass, data leakage, material fraud pattern, unexplained reversal spike, or economics outside the pre-approved loss limit.

## Conditional paid-acquisition plan

### Current state

Paid acquisition is zero. There are no search, social, display, creator, affiliate, retargeting, custom-audience, or lookalike campaigns. No advertising platform receives prototype events, product views, testing lookups, cart contents, email addresses, or audience data.

### Activation gates

Paid media remains off until:

- every operating launch gate is passed in writing;
- counsel approves the exact platform, account, jurisdiction, audience, keyword, negative keyword, creative, landing page, offer, and conversion event;
- the platform independently accepts the exact business and campaign without a misleading account category;
- approved organization-level conversion events can be measured without health-intent, product-interest, or sensitive-data transmission;
- landing-page claims and report states have owners, sources, review dates, and rapid withdrawal controls;
- fraud, support, complaint, refund, quality-event, and attribution operations are staffed;
- a conservative contribution-margin model establishes a maximum allowable acquisition cost and a fixed pilot loss limit.

### Pilot structure

Start with one approved platform, one jurisdiction, one qualified-organization audience, one neutral landing page, and two materially different claim-reviewed creative concepts. Brand and neutral evidence-literacy or procurement-workflow intent may be tested only if counsel and platform review the actual query set. Product, consumer outcome, treatment, weight loss, body transformation, dosing, preparation, and personal-use intent remain excluded.

Use exact-match or tightly controlled allowlists first, daily search-term review, conservative frequency caps, campaign-level UTMs, and first-party aggregate reporting. Do not use product-page or cart retargeting, customer-list lookalikes, data-broker segments, modeled health audiences, audience expansion, or automated creative that can drift beyond approved language.

Release budget in capped tranches:

1. **Integrity test:** validate placement, query, audience, landing-page, consent, and event accuracy without optimizing for volume.
2. **Qualification test:** evaluate cost per completed and accepted organization qualification against the fixed loss limit.
3. **Incrementality test:** compare against an approved holdout or matched baseline; do not rely on platform-reported attribution alone.
4. **Scale review:** expand only after claims, privacy, qualification quality, support load, unit economics, and incrementality all pass the predeclared threshold.

### Paid measurement and stop rules

Primary metrics are approved landing-page completion, qualified-organization start, accepted qualification, incremental accepted-account cost, downstream contribution after exceptions, and blended acquisition cost. Guardrails are prohibited-query rate, consumer-intent rate, claim incidents, privacy incidents, platform disapprovals, qualification rejection, complaint rate, fraud, support load, and quality events.

Stop the affected campaign immediately for:

- any human-use, outcome, personal-health, preparation, dosing, or misleading query/creative match;
- unapproved geography, audience expansion, placement, landing page, or automated copy;
- a platform-policy or regulator concern;
- an unsupported claim, stale evidence state, or altered report-to-item relationship;
- transmission of product, cart, search, testing, intended-use, or inferred-health data;
- qualification bypass, adverse audience drift, or the pre-approved loss limit being reached.

No spend increase occurs automatically. Restart or scaling requires documented review by legal, claims, privacy/security, finance, and operating owners.

## Cross-channel attribution and governance

- Use neutral campaign identifiers; never place molecule names, conditions, protocols, intended use, or other health-interest signals in URLs, UTMs, pixels, CRM fields, or partner links.
- Keep product/search/cart/testing behavior inside the first-party browser-local analytics boundary unless a later data-protection review explicitly approves a narrower aggregate event.
- Use a documented source-of-truth event dictionary and reconcile first-party, platform, email, referral, qualification, and finance totals without identity stitching across unrelated contexts.
- Count an accepted qualified organization—not a click, product view, demo add, or cart—as the earliest future acquisition outcome.
- Review channel, landing-page, claim, privacy, audience, and economics performance weekly during any pilot.
- Require human approval for new queries, creatives, audiences, partners, lifecycle flows, and budget tranches.
- Preserve a universal kill switch for email, referral, and paid systems and test it before activation.
