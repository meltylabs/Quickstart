# OVO Labs — Competitive Intelligence

*Research refresh: July 24, 2026*

## Executive conclusion

There is no public basis for naming a “highest-converting peptide website.” Comparable traffic quality, conversion definitions, refunds, cohort retention, and revenue data are not public.

The defensible conclusion is narrower:

- American Peptides has the most complete observable direct-response merchandising system in the reviewed set.
- Modern Peptides has the clearest observable document-state architecture.
- Protide makes verification and fulfillment confidence unusually prominent.
- Pure U.S. Peptides uses education and technical depth to support product decisions.
- Skye Peptides visibly emphasizes access control.
- Loti Labs and Core Peptides demonstrate the acquisition value—and UX cost—of broad catalogs.
- Established scientific suppliers provide the best long-term model for taxonomy and document retrieval.

OVO Labs should combine those mechanics with independent ecommerce evidence, then validate the combination through its own instrumentation.

## Evidence-quality rubric

| Grade | Meaning | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|
| A | Official regulator, law, or directly observable first-party interface fact | State as fact with date and scope |
| B | Independent research with disclosed methodology or large-scale benchmark | Use as strong design input |
| C | Vendor-, platform-, or agency-published case study | Directional hypothesis; do not generalize the lift |
| D | Anonymous forum, review, estimate, or unverified secondary claim | Vocabulary and idea generation only |
| H | OVO Labs internal hypothesis or model assumption | Test before treating as true |

## Current direct-response benchmark: American Peptides

The July 24, 2026 public homepage visibly includes:

- persistent Shop, Bundles, COA, Learn, partner, wholesale, account, search, and cart paths;
- category navigation and product counts;
- product cards with prices and Add actions;
- bundles and adjacent lab supplies;
- claim-dense trust modules;
- report and SDS links near product identity;
- FAQs, lifecycle mechanics, and service assertions.

This makes it the clearest public reference for merchandising completeness, not for legal safety, evidence validity, or conversion leadership. OVO Labs should transfer its product-finding completeness while rejecting claims it cannot support, use-adjacent supplies, fabricated social proof, and high-density promotional clutter.

**Evidence grade: A for visible interface features; low/unknown for actual conversion performance.**

## Independent ecommerce evidence

| Finding | Source | Grade | OVO Labs implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 58% of desktop and 78% of mobile ecommerce sites in Baymard’s 2025 benchmark had poor-to-mediocre product-list UX | [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-product-list-and-filtering) | B | Product lists, filters, sorting, applied state, and thumbnails deserve product-level rigor |
| Exposing mobile search was associated with +3.34% conversion and +9.93% RPV for Oaktree Memorials | [Blend Commerce](https://blendcommerce.com/blogs/our-work/oaktree-memorials-shopify-cro-case-study-results-after-6-months) | C | Keep search exposed on mobile; run an OVO Labs-specific test |
| Mobile collection discovery variants were associated with +28% conversion and +12% RPV | Same Oaktree case | C | Use visual category entry and simple collection orientation |
| Expanded PDP accordion navigation was associated with +13% RPV and +20% AOV | Same Oaktree case | C | Use concise accordions below a complete first-screen decision block |
| Adding incomplete star ratings to collection cards reduced RPV by 10% in one Gym King test | [Shoplift](https://www.shoplift.ai/case-studies/gym-king) | C | Keep ratings absent until coverage and verification exist |
| Gym King reports a simplified cart, two complete products on mobile, and load time improvement from ~4s to ~2s within a broader optimization program | Same Gym King case | C | Design dense mobile cards, simplify the drawer, and treat speed as conversion work |
| Nuvemshop reports healthy LCP rising from 57% to 96%; the same mobile-organic cohort saw +8.9% conversion and +8.4% cart engagement | [web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/nuvemshop) | B/C | Prioritize above-fold media, remove its lazy loading/transitions, and add explicit fetch priority |
| Baymard reports many stores omit essential filter/sort behavior and sufficient product thumbnails | [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-product-list-and-filtering) | B | Keep filters useful, visible, combinable, and resettable; show product imagery consistently |

Case-study percentages are not additive and are not forecasts.

## Regulatory intelligence

### What is established

- FDA’s March 31, 2026 Gram Peptides warning letter states that “research use only” labels did not overcome website evidence of human intended use. It also treated bacteriostatic water sold beside peptides as evidence of intended injection use. **Grade A.**
- FDA’s current GLP-1 page states that retatrutide and cagrilintide cannot be used in compounding under federal law and have not been found safe and effective for any condition. **Grade A.**
- FDA warning letters to USApeptide.com, Summit Research Peptides, and other sellers repeat the principle that the full marketing context matters. **Grade A.**

### OVO Labs implication

The fiction banner is not a future compliance system. OVO Labs must keep the current concept noncommercial and free of outcome, dosing, administration, preparation, and human-use signals. Any operating model needs advice on the exact product, copy, audience, data flow, jurisdiction, and transaction.

## Pattern library

### Transfer now

| Pattern | Why | OVO Labs execution |
|---|---|---|
| Exposed search | Serves directed intent immediately | Persistent input, predictive name/code/category results |
| Visual categories | Reduces choice overload | Four clear category tiles and subnavigation |
| Dense product cards | Speeds comparison | Complete card anatomy; four-up desktop, two-up mobile |
| Product-adjacent evidence state | Answers uncertainty in context | Testing micro-row on cards and full state on PDP |
| Predictable PDP anatomy | Lowers cognitive load | Same decision block and accordion order on every PDP |
| Document-state taxonomy | Prevents false equivalence | Planned, no result reported, pending, reported, reviewed, rejected, superseded, withdrawn |
| Simple cart drawer | Preserves context | Local add/edit/remove/clear with no checkout |
| Fast above-fold media | Improves experience quality | WebP, dimensions, no lazy-load above fold, high priority |

### Transfer only after proof exists

- reviews and ratings;
- laboratory names and logos;
- purity or test-result claims;
- customer counts;
- fulfillment times;
- support-response metrics;
- bestseller or popularity labels;
- availability and inventory;
- returns or replacement promises.

### Reject

- fake scarcity or countdowns;
- human-outcome testimonials;
- before/after imagery;
- preparation kits or use-adjacent supplies;
- euphemisms intended to hide product identity or intended use;
- unrelated or stale reports beside a product;
- “every batch tested” without a traceable batch relationship;
- loyalty or replenishment framed around personal use;
- hidden search on a technical catalog;
- dead controls used as visual decoration.

## Competitive whitespace

OVO Labs’ open position is:

> Direct-response product finding with scientific restraint.

The category often optimizes one of four things:

1. acquisition breadth;
2. promotional conversion;
3. technical depth;
4. access restriction.

OVO Labs’ concept is the intersection of:

- acquisition-grade search;
- compact ecommerce comparison;
- honest testing-state design;
- restrained copy;
- performance and accessibility.

## Strategic risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Response |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Copying category aesthetics without the operating proof | High | High | Separate visible pattern from underlying claim |
| Assuming a polished competitor is the conversion leader | High | Medium | Treat public UX as observable, performance as unknown |
| Overbuilding catalog breadth before search data | Medium | Medium | Keep the concept to 10 products |
| Using social proof with incomplete coverage | Medium | High | No reviews until real, verified, and sufficiently complete |
| Performance regressions from merchandising modules | Medium | High | Enforce budgets and monitor p75 field data |
| Intended-use contradiction | High in this category | Critical | Noncommercial concept; counsel gates any future activation |
