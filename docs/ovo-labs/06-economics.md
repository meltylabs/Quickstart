# OVO Labs — Economics Assumptions

*Decision model only. OVO Labs has no revenue, customers, orders, inventory, cost of goods, or validated demand.*

## Two separate models

### Model 1 — Concept economics

The current website is evaluated as a product prototype:

- cost to research, design, build, test, and maintain;
- speed and quality of the learning generated;
- whether users can complete product-finding and cart tasks;
- whether the concept can be reused as an ecommerce system.

### Model 2 — Conditional operating economics

Only after all launch gates pass, a separate model may evaluate actual material, testing, compliance, fulfillment, and revenue.

The two models must never be blended. A demo-cart subtotal is not demand, revenue, inventory value, or a forecast.

## Concept learning metrics

| Metric | Formula | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Search-to-PDP rate | Search result selections ÷ search submissions | Directed discovery |
| Category-to-PDP rate | PDP views from category ÷ category entries | Browse quality |
| Zero-result rate | Zero-result searches ÷ search submissions | Taxonomy debt |
| Product-card CTR | PDP views from cards ÷ visible-card sessions | Card completeness |
| Testing engagement | Testing opens ÷ PDP views | Evidence demand |
| Demo-add rate | Demo adds ÷ PDP or card viewers | Directional merchandising intent |
| Cart-open rate | Cart opens ÷ demo-add sessions | Cart discoverability |
| Truth comprehension | Correct fiction responses ÷ test participants | Non-negotiable guardrail |
| Cost per validated learning | Research spend ÷ accepted learning decisions | Prototype efficiency |

No concept metric is translated into revenue without a separately justified model.

## Conditional unit-economics variables

If commerce is ever authorized, calculate contribution per actual order:

```text
net revenue
- refunds and chargebacks
- material landed cost
- independent testing allocation
- receiving and custody
- pick, pack, storage, and freight
- payment and fraud cost
- customer support
- quality-event reserve
- recall/withdrawal reserve
= contribution before payroll and overhead
```

## Illustrative conditional scenarios

| Variable | Conservative | Base | Strong |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Qualified active accounts | 15 | 30 | 60 |
| Orders per account/month | 0.7 | 1.0 | 1.4 |
| Average order value | $350 | $550 | $850 |
| Product gross margin before quality/fulfillment | 30% | 42% | 52% |
| Testing and quality allocation/order | $95 | $80 | $65 |
| Fulfillment, payment, support/order | $70 | $60 | $55 |
| Exception reserve as % revenue | 5% | 3% | 2% |

### Base-case arithmetic

- 30 accounts × 1.0 order × $550 = $16,500 monthly gross revenue.
- 42% preliminary gross margin = $6,930.
- 30 orders × $140 testing, quality, fulfillment, payment, and support = $4,200.
- 3% exception reserve = $495.
- Illustrative contribution = $2,235 before payroll, insurance, legal, software, tax, and working-capital cost.

This model demonstrates fragility; it does not predict an operating result.

## Break-even logic

```text
monthly break-even orders
= fixed monthly operating cost
÷ contribution per order after all variable costs and reserves
```

If fixed operating cost were $35,000 and contribution per order were $150, break-even would require approximately 234 orders per month. That volume would then need to be tested against qualified demand, quality capacity, working capital, and risk—not merely traffic.

## Working-capital assumptions

Any future model must include:

- supplier deposits and lead time;
- testing and release hold time;
- rejected or unusable material;
- inventory aging;
- shipping and customs delay;
- refunds, disputes, and processor reserves;
- tax timing;
- quality-event and recall cash;
- backup supplier and laboratory capacity.

No owner distribution occurs before monthly close and reserve funding.

## Price architecture

The current $45–$118 item prices and $82–$169 concept-set prices are merchandising assumptions used to test:

- card and PDP price readability;
- sorting;
- cart arithmetic;
- set-versus-item comparison;
- perceived category structure.

They do not come from a supplier quote, landed-cost model, willingness-to-pay study, or approved catalog.

Any future price needs:

```text
landed item cost
+ test and release allocation
+ fulfillment and payment
+ support allocation
+ exception/recall reserve
+ fixed-cost contribution
+ risk-adjusted target margin
= permitted price
```

## Acquisition model

Paid acquisition remains zero in the concept stage.

If a future model is approved, the maximum allowable customer-acquisition cost is constrained by realized contribution, not gross revenue:

```text
allowable CAC
<= expected 12-month contribution
× risk-adjusted payback fraction
```

Use cohort retention, refunds, support burden, and quality costs. Never apply general ecommerce LTV multipliers to an unvalidated, regulated category.

## Sensitivity priorities

Model these variables first because they can reverse the business case:

1. lawful addressable catalog;
2. independently verified qualified demand;
3. testing and rejection cost;
4. processor and insurance cost;
5. supplier minimums and lead time;
6. contribution per order;
7. reorder frequency;
8. exception, withdrawal, and recall reserve;
9. working-capital cycle;
10. customer-support and evidence-delivery burden.

## Kill criteria

- Unit economics only work when testing, support, insurance, reserves, or rejection costs are omitted.
- Model assumes inventory sells at list value.
- Demand is inferred solely from demo adds or competitor traffic.
- Banking or processing cost assumes a misleading category.
- Qualified demand cannot cover the evidence and compliance layer.
- Working capital depends on immediate sell-through.
- Profitability depends on prohibited claims or consumer personal-use demand.
